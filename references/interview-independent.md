# Track C — Independent

## Aim

Reveal which parts of the user's business performance are repeatable, which depend excessively on the owner, and which could become better positioning, productized services, higher-value offers, content, or scalable assets.

## Track-Specific Questions

Select and adapt questions according to the shared modules; do not ask all mechanically.

### Business reality

- What currently creates revenue, what do you personally handle, and how long has it operated?
- Which customer problem are people actually paying to solve?

### Actions and results

- What three actions most contributed to reaching the current result?
- Choose the strongest one: what was true before, what did you change, and what happened afterward?
- Which result has repeated enough that it is unlikely to be luck?

### Acquisition and retention

- How do customers first discover you, and what proportion comes from each source?
- Why do customers return, refer, upgrade, or leave?
- Which part of acquisition or delivery depends so heavily on you that growth creates exhaustion?

### External value signals

- What do customers, peers, suppliers, or former colleagues ask you to explain or fix?
- Has anyone asked for a paid service, proposal, partnership, introduction, template, or training? Separate requests from compliments.

### Relative advantage

- Which business problem do you recognize faster than peers, and what signals reveal it?
- What repetitive work can you sustain or systematize that others abandon?

### Assets and experiments

- Which audience, relationships, location, content library, equipment, reputation, tools, or data could support a low-cost test?
- If several projects are active, what are their time cost, current signal, learning value, and path to revenue?

### Constraints and goal

- What single 90-day result matters most: process efficiency, first revenue, repeat sales, better margin, less owner dependence, or a validated offer?
- What weekly time and monthly cash can be spent without harming the core business or health?

## Opportunity Emphasis

Compare improvements to the core business, productized services, retainers, advisory work, digital products, content or intellectual property, partnerships, and carefully bounded experiments. Respect conflicts with current customers or competitors. Do not recommend selling training merely because people ask questions when the user dislikes teaching.
