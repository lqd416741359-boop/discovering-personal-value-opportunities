# Track B — Unemployed or Changing Careers

## Aim

Translate prior experience into credible evidence for a new market while balancing near-term income, learning time, confidence, and transition risk.

## Track-Specific Questions

Select and adapt questions according to the shared modules; do not ask all mechanically.

### Experience inventory

- Across paid work, family responsibilities, volunteering, hobbies, and informal projects, which situations required real responsibility?
- What have you done repeatedly even though the job titles or settings were different?

### Transferable evidence

- Which past result would make a skeptical employer or client believe you can perform in a new field?
- What did you personally do, and what artifact, number, reference, or demonstration could support it?
- Who has relied on you, returned for help, or recommended you?

### Direction and learning

- Which target roles or problems attract you, and what part is interest versus informed experience?
- What have you learned unusually quickly before, and how did you prove that learning in practice?

### Near-term security

- How many weeks or months can you safely experiment before income becomes urgent?
- Do you need the fastest acceptable income, the strongest long-term path, or a staged combination?

### Constraints

- Which location, caregiving, health, education, language, schedule, or confidence barriers are real?
- What will you not spend, borrow, disclose, or risk during the transition?

## Opportunity Emphasis

Compare adjacent roles, bridge roles, portfolio projects, contract work, productized services, and short cash-flow experiments. Do not present a major reskilling path as quick income. Do not treat a course certificate as proof of employable performance without applied evidence.
