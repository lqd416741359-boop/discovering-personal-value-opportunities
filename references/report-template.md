# Final Report Contract

Use the user's language unless asked otherwise. Be concise enough to act on and detailed enough to trace conclusions to evidence.

## 1. Central value pattern

State in one or two sentences the combination of abilities, evidence, and context that appears most valuable. Avoid generic labels such as “hard-working” without explaining what the persistence produces.

## 2. Evidence status

State whether the report used only the interview or also used live research. Note important missing evidence and confidence limits.

## 3. Evidence-backed strengths

For each of three to five strengths include:

- strength name;
- score out of 15;
- supporting facts and external signals;
- transferability;
- limitation or missing proof.

## 4. Top three opportunities

Use a comparison table with personal fit, demand evidence, startup cost, speed to revenue, sustainability, risk, total, and evidence status. Explain why the ranking may differ from the raw total when a strategic dependency matters.

For each opportunity state:

- target user or buyer;
- problem and proposed value;
- evidence already available;
- assumptions still unverified;
- smallest useful experiment.

## 5. Four-zone growth map

### Already doing well — amplify

Name the behaviors already supported by results. Explain how to make them more reusable or less dependent on effort.

### Worth deeper exploration

List the promising patterns that need another story, metric, comparison, or market signal.

### Weak, missing, or overextended

State candidly where the user lacks positioning, data, proof, focus, repeatability, boundaries, or a revenue mechanism. Distinguish “not yet evidenced” from “not capable.”

### Best three follow-up conversations

Offer three specific deep-dive topics. The user may select one or end the session.

## 6. Thirty-, sixty-, and ninety-day validation plan

For each phase include:

- goal;
- actions;
- time and cash cap;
- evidence to collect;
- success, weak-signal, and stop thresholds.

Do not promise that 90 days will produce income. When income is a goal, include a process milestone and a commercial milestone.

## 7. Risks and non-recommendations

State what the user should not do yet and why. Include privacy, employment, legal, financial, health, or owner-dependency risks when relevant.

## Tone Check

The ending should feel like a clear-eyed friend: recognize what is already working, name blind spots without humiliation, and identify the next useful question. Do not end with generic encouragement.
