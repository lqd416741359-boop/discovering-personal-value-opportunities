# Track A — Employed

## Aim

Find underused value that can improve the current role, support promotion or an internal move, strengthen an external job search, or enable a low-risk side experiment without breaching employment duties.

## Track-Specific Questions

Select and adapt questions according to the shared modules; do not ask all mechanically.

### Experience and responsibility

- What are you officially responsible for, and what do people rely on you for that is not in the job description?
- Which earlier role or life experience still shapes how you solve problems today?

### Outcomes and recognition

- Think of one moment in the last year when your work clearly exceeded the basic expectation. What changed, and what evidence shows it?
- Who asks for your help across teams, and what happens after they use your input?
- When have you been trusted with a difficult client, deadline, system, negotiation, or recovery?

### Relative advantage

- What recurring task drains colleagues but feels understandable or manageable to you?
- Which problem do you notice earlier than others, and what cues do you use?

### Career options

- If your current job disappeared, which results could you prove to another employer without using confidential material?
- Would the next 90 days be more valuable for promotion, transfer, external search, skill proof, or a side experiment?

### Constraints and safety

- What do your contract, confidentiality duties, conflict-of-interest rules, health, and available time rule out?
- What income or benefit must remain protected while testing alternatives?

## Opportunity Emphasis

Compare internal expansion, promotion evidence, adjacent roles, external roles, portfolio proof, and low-conflict side projects. Never recommend disclosing employer information, competing improperly, or resigning before an option is validated and financially safe.
